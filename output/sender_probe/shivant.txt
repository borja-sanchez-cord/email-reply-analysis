Hey Team,

Following up on yesterday's meeting with Barr and Idan, I wanted to
circulate some key points about the tooling for internal consideration.

*How Video Management Works*: The platform is designed to manage videos
natively from curation through to evaluation. Thus, there is no need to
register every single frame for curation as there is the ability to access
the video asset in the interface. Curation can be performed on both the
video and frame-level, with the native video being sent directly to
annotation. For clarity, Clients are only charged on frames selected for
sampling (by defining key frames or frames at a fixed interval). The
credits provided to UVEye will only count frames marked to be viewed
individually, which roll up to your overall capacity allocation.

*Impact on Your Workflow*: As an example, you'll register the original
video clips and define a fixed interval of one frame per second for
frame-level curation. This gives you granular insights while annotation
teams access the entire asset for context from previous and future frames.
The result is higher quality data with better cost efficiency, as fewer
frame credits are consumed per video while retaining access to complete
assets.

*BigQuery Integration Status*: The BigQuery integration with Toyota is
still a work in progress with unconfirmed timelines. I'd suggest we park
this option for now and revisit later, since Index Explorer will be far
better suited for your needs in terms of speed, collaboration, and scale.

resources to showcase the flow of video-native curation, annotation, and
