Encord is a development platform for multimodal AI. Teams use us to
annotate, curate, and evaluate training data across images, video, point
clouds, and depth: the modalities that sit at the core of robot perception.
Given your focus on grasp pose estimation, IMO segmentation, and surface
reconstruction, a few things tend to resonate with research engineers
working on similar problems:

   1. Multi-view and 3D-native labeling. Native support for synchronized
   RGB-D, LiDAR, and multi-camera rigs, with 3D cuboids, point cloud
   segmentation, and surface annotation: useful when ground truth has to be
   consistent across views.
   2. Closing the learning-from-interaction loop. Workflows for ingesting
   interaction data, surfacing high-uncertainty or failure cases, and routing
   them back into labeling or active learning queues.
   3. Data curation at scale. Embedding-based search and quality metrics to
   find edge cases (rare objects, unusual grasps, occlusions) inside large
   unlabeled robotics datasets.
   4. Model evaluation. Comparing model versions on slices of data, e.g.,
   performance by object category, lighting, or scene complexity, without
   rebuilding eval harnesses each time.

Happy to walk through how a few teams working on manipulation and
perception are using the platform, such as Fox Robotics and Dexterity.
