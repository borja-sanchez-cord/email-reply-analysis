Cold email reply study · Encord · Jan 2025 – Jul 2026

What Gets a Reply

We took 12,077 outreach emails, measured everything countable about them, had a blinded AI rate their quality, and asked which traits go with getting a reply. Then we did the one thing that separates findings from stories: we wrote our answers down, sealed away the 2026 data, and tested every claim on it — once.

Finding 1 · Confirmed on sealed data

Mass-produced emails get a quarter of the replies

Send the same body to 3+ people and the reply rate collapses — within the same sender, so it isn't “worse reps use templates.” This was the largest effect in 2025 and it repeated almost exactly in 2026.

Cold pitches, reply rate. 2026 tested blind: predicted before looking, p=0.005. Same result on the stricter “showed real interest” outcome (0.9% vs 5.6% in 2026).

Finding 2 · Confirmed on sealed data

A real reason to write now roughly doubles replies

A launch, a funding round, an event, a referral, a visit — something checkable that explains “why this email, this week.” This beat every quality the AI judged, both years. Doing research on the prospect did not matter; having a reason to write did.

Cold pitches, reply rate. 2026: +2.9 points (p=0.047 replied, p=0.012 interested), inside the band predicted in advance, ranked #1 again as predicted.

Just as important

What does nothing

These contradict most sales-blog advice. Each was tested with the same rigor as the findings above — “no effect” here is an answer, not an absence.

Doesn't hurt. If anything, helped (+4.9), but not solidly enough to claim.

Going even shorter than 100 words
No extra gain. Under 100 is a floor, not a slope.

Reply rate by email length: the benefit is a floor at ~100 words, not “shorter is always better.”

The safety net, working

Two findings died on fresh data — that's the system doing its job

“Recipient-focused emails win” looked great in 2025 (+5.3 points, passed every internal check). On sealed 2026 data: −0.4. Dead. It was labelled exploratory with warnings attached, and every warning turned out to be earned. Without the sealed-data test, this would be in the recommendations.

“Bullet points ruin event invites” (−9.6 in 2025) came back at +1.3 in 2026. A one-year artefact, now on record as such.

How we made sure this is real

Five safeguards

Rules first
Every claim and its pass/fail rule was written down and timestamped in git before the answer was known.
Blind judging
The AI that rated email quality never saw who wrote an email, when, or whether it got a reply.
Two judges
A second, stronger AI re-scored 1,000 emails. Where they couldn't agree, the quality was thrown out.
Shuffle test
The whole analysis was re-run on deliberately scrambled data 10 times. It correctly found ~nothing.
The 2026 exam
Half the data stayed sealed until the very end, then every prediction was tested on it exactly once.

Fine print, in plain words

What this study can and can't say

  • Scope. This covers email with a Gmail record — hand-sent and Apollo-sent. Amplemarket blasts are invisible to us (their replies can't be tracked reliably), and they grew from 20% of outreach in 2025 to 71% in 2026. So these findings describe a shrinking, more careful slice of outbound — about 75% of it in 2025, under 30% in 2026 — and say nothing about whether the Amplemarket machine works.
  • The sending tool doesn't change what works. Tool-sent emails reply about 2 points lower across the board, but it's a flat offset: across all 29 traits tested, the tool changed the effect of essentially none of them. It shifts the baseline, not the recipe — and it's likely partly a tracking artefact, so no claim is made either way.
  • Correlation, mostly. Reps choose who gets the hand-written email. Comparing each rep against themselves removes a lot of that, but a rep may save their best emails for their best prospects. Treat effect sizes as upper bounds.
  • Two AI qualities were thrown out. “Economy” and “peer tone” couldn't be scored consistently — by either of two different AIs — so nothing is claimed about them.
  • The personalisation nulls are honest nulls, not proofs. Two years, never a significant positive effect — but 2026 was too small to certify the effect is under 3 points. “We found nothing, twice” is the exact claim.
  • One known leak. 155 Amplemarket emails slipped into scope on a technicality. Documented, left in place, and every 2026 test was re-run without them — nothing changes.
