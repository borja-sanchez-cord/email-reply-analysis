What Gets a Reply

Pre-registered observational study, n = 12,077 outreach emails. 2025 was used as the training set; Jan–Jul 2026 was held back as a sealed validation set and opened once.

1.2 · Design principles

  1. P1 — Within-sender comparison. Every effect compares a rep's own emails with vs without a trait; rep skill and rep targeting cancel out.
  2. P2 — Rules before results. Traits, thresholds, outcome definitions and pass/fail rules were committed to a timestamped record before any result was computed.
  3. P3 — Multiplicity control. ~40 traits tested ⇒ 1–2 false positives expected by chance; Benjamini–Hochberg correction applied within pre-declared families.
  4. P4 — Blind judging. The AI judge saw subject + body only: sender, date, recipient identity and outcome redacted. Automated leak gate: 0 leaks / 12,462 items.
  5. P5 — Placebo. The full analysis re-run on outcome-shuffled data ×10: 5 spurious hits in 240 tests (2.1%), under the 5% ceiling. The machinery invents nothing.
  6. P6 — Training / validation split. 2025 = training set (free exploration); Jan–Jul 2026 = validation set, sealed until every prediction was committed, then opened exactly once. A finding that fails validation is withdrawn, never rescued.

3.1 · Statements

  1. Follow-up 1 out-performs the opener in both years (5.0 vs 4.1; 6.0 vs 5.8). Predicted; HELD.
  2. 61% of all replies arrive after the opener; ~90% of eventual replies are in by touch 3.
  3. Touches 5–8 added 0.6 pp combined (2025). Predicted ≥85% of replies by touch 4; observed 96% (2026); HELD.
  4. Constraint: who receives touch 5 is the rep's choice — late-touch rates describe survivors.

Section 7 · Validity boundaries

7 · What this study cannot say

  1. L1 — Scope. Gmail-recorded outbound only (hand-sent + Apollo). Amplemarket sends have no reliable reply trail and are excluded; they grew from 20% of outreach (2025) to 71% (2026). Findings cover ~75% of 2025 outbound but <30% of 2026's, and say nothing about the Amplemarket channel.
  2. L2 — Sending tool. Tool-sent email replies ~2 pp lower, but the offset is flat: 2 of 29 trait-effects differ by route (the chance rate). It shifts baselines, not the recipe; likely partly a tracking artefact. No claim made.
  3. L3 — Causality. Within-sender comparison removes rep-level differences, not within-rep targeting (a rep may save better emails for better prospects). Effect sizes are upper bounds; directions are the trustworthy part. R1/R2 are the natural A/B-test candidates.
  4. L4 — R2's 2026 margin. Confirmed at p=0.047 under the pre-committed rule; two of five robustness specifications lift p above 0.05 (sign always holds; the fair-window outcome strengthens it to p=0.024). Reported as confirmed with a fragile margin.
  5. L5 — Power. 2026 is ¼ of 2025; four verdicts are inconclusive by design honesty, not evidence of absence.
  6. L6 — Known residuals. 155 Amplemarket emails in scope on a technicality (all analyses re-run without them; nothing changes); 4 test emails in frame (0.03%); 55 dedup boundary pairs of 224,756 touches; ~0.1% replies missed via colleague hand-offs; 0.5% non-English emails judged with an English rubric; July 2026 reply windows clipped (97% of replies arrive ≤30 days; the fair-window sensitivity strengthens both confirmations).
